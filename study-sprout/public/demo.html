BEFORE · WORKFLOW HYPOTHESIS

Reread, ask, rebuild.

A child rereads notes, asks a parent what to study, and separately maintains a checklist or calendar. A missed evening or quiz mistake means deciding what to revisit and rebuilding the plan.

Customer interviews and the frequency and severity of this problem remain unvalidated.

AFTER · DEMONSTRABLE WORKFLOW

Notes → recall → a revised plan.

Paste notes, set a test date and daily time budget, and open the next task. Answer a short recall quiz with confidence, then preview a revised schedule that prioritizes confident mistakes within available time. If work cannot fit, the app surfaces the capacity limit.

Proposed pilot KPI: at least 30% lower median planning time.

A target, not an achieved result. Compare each family’s usual workflow with the app: time from opening study materials to choosing the first specific task across three baseline evenings and three app evenings per family. Record parent reminders and first-use failures alongside timing.

04 / TECHNICAL EXECUTION & DEMO PROOF 20%

Open it. Try it. Check the evidence.

Walkthrough on the public app

Open Study Sprout. If a plan already exists, choose New study plan, then Use fictional sample notes.

  1. From the empty state, choose Try a fictional sample plan. Review the science notes, test date and daily budget, then select Build my study plan.
  2. Select Take my next step, read the topic summary and source evidence, then explicitly choose Mark session complete.
  3. Choose Practice now. Select an answer and confidence, then Check answer. Read the explanation and evidence. Use Next question and finish with See my results to save the attempt.
  4. Return with Back to dashboard, then choose Rebalance my plan. Preview the schedule and its daily totals before applying. Confident mistakes guide review priority; impossible schedules are blocked without silently dropping work.
  5. Refresh to verify saved plans and completion. An unfinished quiz attempt is not saved. Export notes or the plan for a readable copy; exports are not restorable backups.

Hosted adaptation, transparent limits.

The hosted student app uses browser-local rule-based generation with OCI disabled. No live AI call is claimed. The repository additionally contains a Node/OCI implementation and mock/signature tests; those are not evidence of live OCI connectivity.

05 / DIFFERENTIATION & INVESTMENT READINESS 15%

Earn the next experiment.

The differentiation hypothesis.

Parent-paid support for independent study: notes → recall and confidence → an explained next task within a daily budget. Garden focus apps already exist; a garden timer is neither a claimed novelty nor a moat.

Trusted distribution, validated content and consented outcomes could become future advantages. None is a current asset.

Proposed gates, not traction.

At least 30% lower median planning time, and at least 12 of 20 students returning on three distinct study days during the proposed two-week pilot. Both are targets, not results.

Use consented pilot logs; the app has no shared analytics collection. Record first-use failures and parent reminders so timing gains do not hide friction.

If the single-test wedge works:

Expand to recurring tests and more subjects within the same family. Test garden rewards for voluntary return use, then an explicitly priced parent-paid pilot and tutoring-provider referrals. Pricing, retention and distribution remain hypotheses to test.
